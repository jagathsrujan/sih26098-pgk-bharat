02 — ARTILLERY GUN 101

What is an artillery gun and how does it work?

A howitzer is a big cannon that fires indirectly — the shell flies in a high arc (like a basketball shot) over hills and lands tens of km away. The gun crew never sees the target. Unlike a tank gun (flat, direct fire), a howitzer lobs shells at 45°+ elevation.

KILL CHAIN — HOW A FIRING MISSION WORKS
1
Forward Observer — sees target through binoculars/drone, radios: grid AZ123456 + “fire for effect”
2
Fire Direction Centre — ballistic computer calculates range, wind, charge, barrel angle
elevation 42.3° • charge M3A1 • fuze: proximity 12m
3
Gun crew sets fuze → loads SHELL → loads PROPELLANT CHARGE → closes breech → FIRE
4
Shell arcs 20–30km for 60–90 seconds → fuze triggers → detonation at/above target 💥
INSIDE THE GUN — 0 TO 800 m/s IN 17 ms
  1. Propellant ignition: Primer ignites charge (nitrocellulose bags). Pressure spikes to 300–400 MPa (~3,000–4,000 atm).
  2. Shot start: Soft copper rotating band is engraved by barrel's rifling (spiral grooves).
  3. Spin-up: Rifling (≈1 turn in 20 calibers) spins shell to ~250 rev/s — gyroscopic spin keeps nose forward (like an American football).
  4. Muzzle exit: A real flight article sees extreme launch acceleration. The printed exhibition model is not tested for it and must stay outside any launch path.
  5. Recoil: Gun jumps back ~1m; hydro-pneumatic cylinders absorb & return barrel.

04 — THE PHYSICS (WITH FORMULAS)

How a shell flies — and why we can fix it with 4 tiny wings

You don’t need to derive these — you need to quote them in reviews. All numbers use our sim params: M107, m=43.2kg, d=0.155m, v₀=684m/s, QE=45° (simulate_trajectory_155.py:21-31).

4.1 INSIDE THE BARREL — HOW HARD IS THE LAUNCH?
a = v² / (2L)   (from v² = u² + 2as)
a = 684² / (2×6.045) = 467,856 / 12.09 ≈ 38,700 m/s² ≈ 3,950 g (average)
Peak (pressure spike 2.5–4×) → 10,000–15,000 g ← future flight-article reference
Time in barrel
t = 2L/v = 12.09/684 ≈ 17.7 ms
Spin from rifling
n = v/(twist·d) ≈ 220 rev/s ≈ 13,000 rpm
Kinetic energy
½mv² ≈ 10.1 MJ
≈ car 1.5t at 420 km/h — in a fire extinguisher
For the active exhibit: do not claim 15,000g survivability. The physical risk is safe low-speed operation: guarded bearings, retained parts, soft edges, and no launch-compatible interface.
4.2 IN FLIGHT — HOW FAR DOES IT GO?
Vacuum range: R = v₀²·sin2θ / g = 684²·sin90°/9.81 ≈ 47.7 km ← textbook
Real range with drag: ≈ 24 km (drag eats half!)
Drag: F = ½ρv²CdA → at muzzle ≈ 810 N → ~1.9g decel
Cd spikes to ~0.31 near Mach 1 (transonic is worst)
Apogee
h = v₀²sin²θ/2g ≈ 11.9 km
Time of flight
99s vacuum / 70–80s real
long time ⇒ small errors grow big
4.3 WHY DUMB SHELLS MISS — OUR REASON TO EXIST
Muzzle velocity varies ±1–2 m/s (propellant temp) • Unknown wind at 12km • Coriolis + Magnus (50–150m) • Manufacturing asymmetry
Each error grows over ~80s → CEP ≈ 267m at 30km → kill one target = 301 shells = 18.2t + 959 charges
4.4 HOW 4 TINY CANARDS FIX 267m OF ERROR — 20× MARGIN
Canard lift: L = ½ρv²·(CL·S)   with CL·S ≈ 0.005 m² → at q≈287kPa → L≈1.4kN → we cap at 3g
Lateral correction: Δx = ½ a t² = ½×29.4×20² ≈ 5.9 km with 20s guided flight
We have ~6km authority against 267m problem → 20× margin. That’s why it works.
4.5 COMMON CANARD CARRIER — ACTIVE DEMO BASELINE
  1. All four illustrative canards are fixed to one common inner carrier; there are no individual fin hinges or fin motors.
  2. The printed shell and outer housing form the spin-side frame; the carrier is free to rotate relative to it on the coaxial bearing pair.
  3. A shell-side rotor and carrier-side stator demonstrate the power path. One low-voltage torque emulator demonstrates carrier-level control.
  4. The bench model shows relative phase only; it is inert, guarded, low-speed, and not validated for flight or launch loads.
4.6 CEP MATH — WHAT WE REPORT TO JUDGES
Fire N=1000 sims with randomized errors (Monte Carlo). rᵢ = impact distance from target. Sort rᵢ, median → CEP = r₅₀₀ (radius containing 50%). Target ≤30m. Stretch 10m.
