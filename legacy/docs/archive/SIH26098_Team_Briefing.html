Section 2

Artillery Gun 101 — what it is, how it works

Before you can upgrade a shell, you need to understand the thing that throws it.

What is an artillery gun (howitzer)?

A big cannon that fires shells indirectly — the shell flies in a high arc (like a basketball shot) over hills and lands tens of kilometres away. The gun crew never sees the target.

Unlike a tank gun (flat, direct fire), a howitzer lobs shells at 45°+ elevation. Range comes from muzzle velocity + elevation angle, not from pointing at the target.

The kill chain — how a fire mission works

1
Forward Observer sees the target → radios grid coordinates + “fire for effect”.
2
Fire Direction Centre runs the ballistic computer: range, wind, charge, angle.
3
Gun crew sets the fuze → loads SHELL → loads PROPELLANT CHARGE → closes breech → FIRE.
4
Shell arcs 20–30 km for ~60–90 seconds.
5
Fuze triggers → detonation at or above the target.

Inside the gun — 0 to 800 m/s in 17 milliseconds

1 · Propellant ignition

Primer ignites the charge (bags of nitrocellulose). Pressure spikes to ~300–400 MPa — about 3,000–4,000 atmospheres.

2 · Shot start

Pressure pushes the shell; its soft copper rotating band is engraved by the barrel's rifling (spiral grooves).

3 · Spin-up

Rifling (~1 turn in 20 calibers) spins the shell to ~250 rev/s. That gyroscopic spin keeps it stable — like a thrown American football.

4 · Muzzle exit

A real flight article sees extreme launch acceleration. The printed exhibition model is not tested for it and must stay outside any launch path.

5 · Recoil

The gun jumps back ~1 m; hydro-pneumatic cylinders absorb it and return the barrel to battery.

Scope boundary

15,000 g is a future flight-article requirement, not a claim about our printed model. The exhibit uses low-voltage parts, retained components, a guard, and a documented low-speed operating envelope.

Artillery Shell 101 — anatomy + every term you need

A 155 mm high-explosive shell (M107/M795 class) weighs ~43–47 kg. Our kit replaces only the fuze at the nose — everything else stays stock. Hover/tap each part:

Hover a part — every part except the nose stays exactly as YIL makes it today.

The glossary — memorize these, you'll hear them in reviews

CEP (Circular Error Probable)
Radius around the target inside which 50% of shells land. Smaller = better. Unguided ≈267 m at 30 km → our goal ≤30 m.
Muzzle velocity (v₀)
Speed leaving the barrel: 684 m/s (charge M3A1) up to ~827 m/s (max charge).
Ballistic trajectory
Unpowered arc under gravity + drag. Once it leaves the barrel, a dumb shell cannot change anything.
Apogee
Highest point of the arc (~10–12 km at max range).
Quadrant Elevation (QE)
Gun barrel angle; ~45° gives max range.
Drag
Air resistance, F = ½ρv²CdA. Eats about half the ideal range.
Spin stabilization
Rifling spins the shell ~250 rev/s; gyroscopic stiffness keeps the nose forward.
Magnus effect / Coriolis
Small aerodynamic + Earth-rotation forces pushing the shell sideways 50–150 m at long range — part of why dumb shells miss.
Probable Error (PEr / PEd)
Army way of saying scatter in range / deflection.
Fuze
The “nose computer” that decides WHEN the shell detonates. (Not “fuse” — a fuze is a munition device.)
PD (Point Detonating)
Explodes on contact. Superquick <250 µs; Delay 0–60 ms (digs into bunkers first).
Proximity (VT) mode
Radar measures height, explodes in air 6/12/18 m above ground (HOB = Height Of Burst) — lethal against troops in trenches.
Time mode
Explodes at a set time (3.0–199.9 s) — used for cargo / smoke / illumination shells.
S&A (Safe & Arm)
Lock that keeps the fuze SAFE in storage and ARMS only after real launch signatures: setback ≥850 g + spin ≥2900 rpm + distance ≥100 m (STANAG 4187).
Setback
The massive backward acceleration at firing — used by the fuze as “proof we were actually fired”.
EPIAFS / inductive setter
Wireless (no pins) coil that programs the fuze inside the breech: target coords, mode, GPS keys.
MOFA
Multi-Option Fuze for Artillery — the existing Indian fuze family (ECIL/BEL).
PGK (Precision Guidance Kit)
A fuze-shaped module with GPS + steering surfaces that replaces the standard fuze and steers the shell. Our whole project.
Strakes / canards
Small fins near the nose. Ours are FIXED to the de-spun frame; holding that frame at an orientation aims their net lift.
De-spun stator
The steering frame holds all four fixed strakes and rides on ONE 61811 thin-section bearing relative to the spinning shell. The 12-pole ring motor steers it (torque mode) and harvests spin power (regen mode).
GNC
Guidance (where should I go), Navigation (where am I), Control (hold the de-spun frame at the steering orientation).
IMU
Inertial Measurement Unit — accelerometers + gyroscopes.
GNSS / NavIC / GPS
Satellite navigation. NavIC = India's own constellation (L5 band) — sovereign, can't be switched off by anyone else.
FMCW radar
Frequency-Modulated Continuous Wave — tiny radar that measures distance precisely (our proximity fuze).
SWaP-C
Size, Weight, Power and Cost — the four things we keep LOW.
TRL
Technology Readiness Level (1 = paper idea, 9 = in service). Real PGK = TRL 9.
STANAG 2916
NATO standard for fuze threads/wells — screw our kit in and it fits every 155 mm shell & gun.
6DOF
Six Degrees Of Freedom simulation (3 position + 3 rotation) — how we prove CEP without firing.
Monte Carlo
Run the sim 1000× with random wind/sensor errors → get the CEP histogram.
Section 4

The physics of a shell — formulas + one worked example

You don't need to derive these — you need to quote them in reviews. All numbers use our sim's parameters (cad_models/simulate_trajectory_155.py): M107 shell, m = 43.2 kg, d = 0.155 m, v₀ = 684 m/s, QE = 45°.

4.1 Inside the barrel — how hard is the launch?

Average acceleration:   a = v² / (2L)        (v² = u² + 2as, u = 0)
  a = 684² / (2 × 6.045) = 467,856 / 12.09 ≈ 38,700 m/s² ≈ 3,950 g average
  Peak (pressure spike ≈ 2.5–4× average) → 10,000–15,000 g  ← future flight-article reference

Time inside barrel:     t = 2L / v = 12.09 / 684 ≈ 17.7 ms

Spin from rifling:      n = v / (twist × d) = 684 / (20 × 0.155) ≈ 220 rev/s ≈ 13,000 rpm

Kinetic energy:         KE = ½mv² = ½ × 43.2 × 684² ≈ 10.1 MJ
  (≈ a 1.5-tonne car at 420 km/h — in something the size of a fire extinguisher)
For this exhibit: the hard requirement is safe low-speed operation—retained parts, guarded rotation, soft edges, and a nonfunctional adapter. Do not claim launch survivability.

4.2 In flight — how far does it go?

Ideal (vacuum) range:   R = v₀² · sin(2θ) / g
  R = 684² × sin(90°) / 9.81 = 467,856 / 9.81 ≈ 47.7 km   ← physics-class answer

Real range with drag:   ≈ 24 km (drag eats ~half!)
Drag force:             F = ½ ρ v² Cd A
  at muzzle: ½ × 1.225 × 684² × 0.15 × 0.0189 ≈ 810 N → ~1.9 g of deceleration
  (Cd spikes to ~0.31 near Mach 1 — the transonic region is the worst)

Apogee:                 h = v₀² sin²θ / 2g ≈ 11.9 km
Time of flight:         t = 2 v₀ sinθ / g ≈ 99 s (vacuum) / ~70–80 s real
Why this matters: the shell spends ~80 s in the air. Every aiming error has 80 seconds to grow — which is exactly why dumb shells miss (next block).

4.3 Why do dumb shells MISS? (the whole reason we exist)

The gun is aimed by calculation, but reality adds errors:

  • Muzzle velocity varies shot-to-shot (±1–2 m/s from propellant temperature)
  • Wind aloft is unknown (the shell flies 12 km high, through jet stream)
  • Coriolis (Earth rotation) & Magnus (spin × airflow) forces
  • Manufacturing tolerances (mass, asymmetry)
Each error grows with time of flight → CEP ≈ 267 m at 30 km. To reliably kill one target you must fire ~301 shells = 18.2 tonnes + 959 charges. With our kit: 77 shells, 4.5 t.

4.4 How 4 small strakes fix 267 m of error

Strake lift:            L = ½ ρ v² · (CL·S)
  our strake lift-area: CL·S ≈ 0.005 m²  (from sim, canard-equivalent class)
  at q ≈ 287 kPa near muzzle → L ≈ 1.4 kN available → we cap at 3 g

Lateral correction:     Δx = ½ a t²
  with a = 3 g = 29.4 m/s² held for just 20 s of guided flight:
  Δx = ½ × 29.4 × 20² ≈ 5.9 km of correction authority
We have ~6 km of steering authority against a 267 m problem — 20× margin. That is the mathematical reason a PGK works with just 4 fixed strakes on one de-spun frame and one torque machine.

4.5 How steering with a FIXED de-spun frame works (the clever bit)

  • Our active demo has one de-spun stator: all four strakes are fixed to the same inner frame (`PGK_Stator_Body`) and move together.
  • The front/rear display bearings let the carrier rotate relative to the printed outer shell, making the de-spin relationship visible without independent fin hinges.
  • The ring motor feeds rectifier/boost into the 6× 14430 bay in regen mode; the same machine provides carrier-level steering torque.
  • This is a guarded, low-speed, inert demonstration of relative rotation only—not a flight-control or launch-survivability test.

4.6 CEP math (what we report to judges)

Fire N = 1000 sims with randomized errors (Monte Carlo).
rᵢ = distance of impact i from target.
Sort rᵢ, take the median → CEP = r₅₀₀  (radius containing 50%).

Target: CEP ≤ 30 m.   Stretch: 10 m  (real PGK achieved 10 m median in 2015 tests).

Section 8

New-member FAQ

I know nothing about defence. Can I still contribute?
Yes. This is an engineering project: wings, sensors, code, structures. Everything military you need is in Sections 2–4 of this page. Watch the 7 videos and you'll know enough.
Are we building an actual weapon?
No. We build inert demonstrators: CAD, simulation, 3D prints, bench electronics. No energetics, no explosives, ever. The PS is published by the Government of India for exactly this kind of student engineering.
What's the single hardest exhibition risk?
Clear mechanical separation and safe low-speed operation: the carrier must rotate visibly inside the guarded shell without loose parts, sharp edges, or a launch-compatible interface.
What if NavIC/GPS is jammed?
Stretch goal: an LSTM model that predicts the impact point from IMU data alone (<5 m error in published research). Great “innovation” slide.
How much time do I need to give?
~10 h/week for 3.5 weeks. Daily 1–2 h research entries + one weekend build session per week.
