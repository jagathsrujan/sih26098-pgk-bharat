One bolt-on kit turns a dumb 155 mm round into a precision weapon

A Precision Guidance Kit (PGK) that replaces the standard fuze: canard actuation for in-flight correction, GNSS-aided guidance & control, and a multi-mode electronic fuze — fitted to existing shells, surviving gun launch, costing a fraction of a guided round.

The miss we must close: 267 m → 30 m

Same gun, same charge, same shell. Unguided (red), muzzle-velocity, elevation and wind errors scatter the impact across a ~267 m circle. With canard guidance (amber), the round corrects mid-flight onto a ≤30 m circle — a ~9× tighter footprint.

The kit: five organs on a standard shell

The PGK replaces the fuze well on the nose of the existing 155 mm shell — no modification to the body, the fill, or the gun. Everything forward of the driving band is new; everything behind it is the round the Army already buys.

From fire to burst in six events

On-board signal chain — sense → navigate → steer → fuzing

One flight computer closes both loops: the steering loop (left) runs every 100 ms; the fuze logic (right) only arms after safe-and-arm and selects its mode from the pre-fire setting plus terminal cues.
