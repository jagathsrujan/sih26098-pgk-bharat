## 1. Precision guidance kits will play a crucial role in modern warfare (id=298)

In modern warfare, the use of precision strike ammunition is increasing and has become a key factor in gaining operational initiative and winning wars. Precision strikes offer advantages such as minimal collateral damage, reduced ammunition consumption, fewer logistical personnel requirements, and shorter engagement times. These advantages form the fundamental basis for future military operations, making precision-guided weapon systems the inevitable trend in military development.

However, precision strike ammunition is costly and cannot be mass-produced, while the large stocks of conventional ammunition are insufficient to meet the demands of modern warfare. As a result, there has been the development of ballistic correction projectiles that bridge the gap between the two. Ballistic correction projectiles do not require modifications to the original ammunition structure; they only need a precision guidance kit, also known as a "two-dimensional ballistic correction fuze component," installed at the fuze position of the standard ammunition to transform it into guided ammunition. The "precision guidance kit" can detect the attitude and position of the projectile during flight, adjust the flight trajectory, and provide accuracy comparable to precision-guided ammunition.

Precision guidance kits typically use technologies such as GPS/Beidou satellite system guidance and fin control to make real-time corrections to the projectile's flight trajectory, guiding it to the intended target area. These kits also integrate all the functions of a fuze, transforming conventional projectiles into precision-guided munitions.

## 2. The working Principle of Precision Guidance Kit (id=300)

The precision guidance kit (Two-Dimensional Trajectory Correction Fuze Component) is installed at the detonator position on the head of the projectile. It utilizes the rotational motion of the projectile during flight to drive the magnetic torque generator to generate electricity. This controls the component's control surface angle at different angles relative to the projectile's rotation direction (hovering/variable speed), producing lift in different directions to correct the projectile's flight path and achieve trajectory correction objectives.

Viewed from the front of the projectile, the component's head deploys two pairs of fins. One pair of fins, installed in reverse differential mode (labeled "1 and 2"), is referred to as "steering fins," which provide the power for the servo motor rotation. The other pair of fins, installed in the same direction in parallel (labeled 3 and 4), is referred to as "control fins," which provide the corrective control force needed to adjust the projectile.

During the flight of the projectile, the satellite positioning and navigation system on the precision guidance component provides the real-time spatial position of the projectile. The onboard computer then calculates whether the projectile deviates from the predetermined trajectory based on its real-time position. When the projectile deviates from the predetermined trajectory, the onboard computer sends control commands to the servo motor, directing the "control fins" to "hover" at the specified angle. This action provides the corresponding flight control force to the projectile, correcting its path back to the predetermined trajectory and achieving the objective of trajectory correction.

## 8. Magnetic Moment Motor: The Core Power of Leading Precision Guidance Kits (id=307)

The magnetic moment motor is a key component of the projectile guidance kit. Comprised of components such as the airfoil, magnetic cylinder, winding, bearings, and more, its role is not only to provide energy to the system but more importantly to achieve roll attitude control of the airfoil and generate the torque required for trajectory correction.

Its operating principle: the magnetic moment motor is integrated with the airfoil design, controlling the magnetic resistance torque by adjusting the current, thereby precisely controlling the roll attitude of the airfoil. In trajectory correction, the deflecting airfoil generates a deflecting torque, decoupling the airfoil from the projectile's roll, while the correcting airfoil generates lateral force and torque, achieving trajectory correction through the magnetic moment motor. This precision control capability enables projectiles to accurately hit their targets.

## 11. Enhancing Projectile Precision: The Intricacies of Trigger Module Mechanisms (id=312)

The trigger module employs inertia switches to detect when a projectile makes contact with its target. Whether the projectile touches the ground or skims it at a shallow angle, the inertia switch rapidly captures this information and transmits it to the firing control module. The precision and speed of this process directly determine whether the projectile detonates at the optimal moment.

It primarily consists of radial and axial inertia switches working together:
- **Radial switch:** comprises switch housing, impact rod, impact spring, conductive sleeve, and insulating sleeve — a highly sensitive sensing system capturing projectile-target contact signals.
- **Axial switch:** composed of switch housing, inertia pin, inertia spring, copper foil, conductive sleeve, and insulating sleeve — maintains stability amidst complex motion, ensuring accurate data transmission.

## 12. Precision Guided Munitions: The Core Component Ensuring Safe Detonation (id=314)

The ignition control module is the pivotal component ensuring safe detonation of guided munitions:

## 13. How does a precision guidance kit work? (id=316)

The precision guidance kit consists primarily of: the satellite positioning module, the roll angle measurement module, the control unit, the torque motor, the information receiver module, and the power management module.

Once installed on a projectile and loaded with enemy position data, upon firing, the satellite positioning receiver begins to receive signals, providing real-time data on the projectile's position and velocity. The projectile controller processes this information to determine the projectile's trajectory. Simultaneously, the roll measurement module calculates the roll orientation of the fins based on the projectile's velocity. Guided strategies are then formulated to generate guidance commands based on the projectile's trajectory and target location.

These commands, combined with actual roll data from the fins, form control instructions. The fins adjust their roll orientation according to these instructions, modifying the measurement signals received by the controller and establishing a feedback loop for fin adjustment. Changes in fin orientation affect the projectile's flight path, thereby adjusting feedback to the controller for trajectory measurement and forming a trajectory correction loop.
